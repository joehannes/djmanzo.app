# Music-theory teaching on the web, DJ/karaoke marketing, real-life culture, and gamification: intel for the djmanzo v3 website

Researched 2026-10-19 with about 20 web searches and fetches. Many sources are vendor pages or blog coverage; that is noted wherever it matters. Anything I could not source is in a Gaps list and must not be presented as fact on the site.

## (a) Which interactive web explainers teach music concepts well, and how do they teach?

### Takeaway
The best explainers give you a working instrument before any explanation, keep each concept to one control, use real songs as worked examples, and end in a "playground" where you can make something and take it with you. Ableton (Learning Music, Learning Synths, and the new 2026 Ableton Learn) and Chrome Music Lab are the benchmarks. For harmonic mixing, the strongest web content is still mostly static diagrams from Mixed In Key and DJ-education blogs, so an interactive Camelot explainer would fill a real gap.

### Cited Findings
**Ableton Learning Music (2017)**
- A free site that teaches beginners music production in the browser. It covers beats, notes and scales, chords, basslines and song structure, using exercises built from Live-style clips. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2017-05-08-ableton-interactive-lessons.html); [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/amp/learn-music-production-basics-with-abletons-new-interactive-website)
- It takes apart real songs, including Beyoncé's "Single Ladies" and Robert Hood's "Ride", and ends in "The Playground", where you build a track and export it to Ableton Live. — [FACT](https://factmag.com/2017/05/08/ableton-learning-music-website); [XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/?p=27767)
- It was a companion to Ableton's *Making Music* book and has been translated into 19 languages. — [CDM, July 2026](https://cdm.link/2026/07/ableton-learn/)

**Ableton Learning Synths (2019, updated 2022)**
- A two-oscillator monosynth that runs in the browser, with lessons on oscillators, filters, envelopes and LFOs, plus step-by-step rebuilds of classic sounds. — [XLR8R](https://xlr8r.com/?p=121376); [Synthtopia](https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=109409)
- Experienced users can skip the lessons and go straight to presets and sequences. — [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/gear/ableton-learning-synthesis/)
- The 2022 update added recording of up to 60 seconds, a configurable XY pad and dark mode. — [Mixdown](https://mixdownmag.com.au/?p=62466)
- It lives at learningsynths.ableton.com. — [Vintage Synth Explorer](https://www.vintagesynth.com/articles/ableton-steps-help-educate-people-about-synths)

**Ableton Learn (soft-launched July 2026)**
- It replaces embedded videos with simplified interactive versions of Live's clip view and audio tools. The interactive parts are "powered by the same engine that Ableton Move and Note use". — [CDM, 7 July 2026](https://cdm.link/2026/07/ableton-learn/)
- At launch it had a "Building Blocks" lesson, a starter lesson on beats, and a Playground: a very simplified browser sketchpad version of Live. It was built mobile-first and works on mobile and desktop. CDM calls it mostly a preview. — [CDM](https://cdm.link/2026/07/ableton-learn/)

**Chrome Music Lab (Google)**
- Song Maker is a grid you click to place notes and percussion. You can also sing into the microphone or use a MIDI keyboard, then share the song as a link, download it, or embed it. — [Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/news/learning-music-art-science-math-and-more-with-chrome-music-lab); [Midnight Music](https://midnightmusic.com.au/2018/09/50-lesson-ideas-for-the-chrome-music-lab/)
- Rhythm is a percussion grid where you switch how many beats pass before the pattern repeats. Spectrogram shows the frequencies in a sound, from low to high and over time, for instruments, everyday sounds or your own voice. — [Instructables](https://instructables.com/Fun-With-Music-in-Google-Chrome); [Glow Scotland](https://blogs.glowscotland.org.uk/fa/digilearnfalk/2018/03/07/chrome-music-lab/)
- Sources disagree on how many experiments there are (13 or 14). Music teachers have published lesson plans built on them, such as "50 lesson ideas". — [Midnight Music](https://midnightmusic.com.au/2018/09/50-lesson-ideas-for-the-chrome-music-lab/); [SA Department for Education](https://www.education.sa.gov.au/our-learning-sa/learning-home-activities/arts/chrome-music-lab)

**Hooktheory (TheoryTab / Trends)**
- Over 40,000 crowd-sourced chord and melody analyses of popular songs. Each one shows key, tempo, chords and melody on a keyboard, with scale-degree guides and complexity metrics. — [Hooktheory blog](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/?p=2851)
- Trends lets you build a progression and see which chords most often come next in the database, along with songs that use the same progression. Access is free without an account. — [Hooktheory blog](https://hooktheory.com/blog/?p=2246); [Hooktheory guitar chord chart](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/guitar-chord-chart/)
- Because the analyses are crowd-sourced, some are wrong. — [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/news/hook-theory)

**The Camelot wheel / harmonic mixing (Mixed In Key and others)**
- Each key is written as a number from 1 to 12 plus A or B, for example 8A instead of "A minor". From 8A you can mix into 8A, 7A, 9A or 8B. — [Mixed In Key: How to use the Camelot Wheel](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-use-the-camelot-wheel/); [Mixed In Key book](https://mixedinkey.com/Book/How-To-Use-Harmonic-Mixing-2)
- Mixed In Key credits Mark Davis with inventing the wheel. A third-party guide dates it to 2006. This is vendor attribution. — [mixedinkey.com](https://mixedinkey.com/?p=324); [matthewess.com](https://matthewess.com/camelot-wheel-harmonic-mixing-guide/)
- Mixed In Key writes Camelot keys into file metadata that rekordbox, Traktor and Serato can read. KeyFinder is named as a free open-source alternative. — [Dubspot](https://blog.dubspot.com/harmonic-mixing-w-dj-endo-part-1); [Pyramind](https://pyramind.com/dj-tips-tricks-mixing-in-key-shdws)
- Interface-design consequence for djmanzo: the vendor guides are mostly static wheel images plus rules. I found no widely cited interactive web Camelot toy. — [MixPrism 2026 guide](https://mixprism.eu/blog/camelot-wheel-harmonic-mixing-guide); [Mixed In Key](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-use-the-camelot-wheel/)

**Stems and source separation (djay Neural Mix, Fuser)**
- Algoriddim's Neural Mix splits a track in real time into vocals, drums, bass and harmonics. It is a PRO feature, needs an A12-class chip or newer on iOS, and does not work with music streamed from Apple Music or Spotify. — [Algoriddim help: free vs pro](https://help.algoriddim.com/topic/first-steps/free-vs-pro); [Synthtopia](https://synthtopia.com/?p=117471); [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/?p=2759831)
- Fuser (Harmonix, 2020) gives each song four colour-coded stems (blue drums, green bass/synth, yellow lead, red vocals) on a four-slot deck, and automatically matches tempo and key. Its campaign introduces mute, solo, free-play instruments and effects one at a time. — [Vooks](https://vooks.net/fuser-switch-review); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/featured/fuser-harmonix-game-review)
- PC Gamer says the campaign teaches that "mixing is all about peaks and valleys". Prima's criticism: instructions pop up while the game keeps running, so the crowd gets bored while you read. — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/fuser-review); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/featured/fuser-harmonix-game-review)
- Fuser grew out of DropMix, Harmonix's physical card-based mixing game. — [Nintendo World Report](https://nintendoworldreport.com/hands-on-preview/53097)

**Data-driven music essays (The Pudding)**
- Matt Daniels' hip-hop vocabulary piece counts unique words in each artist's first 35,000 lyric words, lets you hover over or select artists, and was updated with Aesop Rock on top at 7,879 unique tokens. — [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/beyond-wu-tang-clan-who-has-hip-hops-richest-vocabulary/); [Internet Scout](https://archives.internetscout.org/r56452)

### Inferences
- The shared pattern behind Ableton, Chrome Music Lab and Fuser is a loop: hear it, change one control, hear the difference, then free play. Ableton Learn's move away from video toward real working tools confirms the trend into 2026.
- Fuser's main lesson is about pacing: never ask someone to read while the music demands their attention. A djmanzo explainer should pause, loop or quieten the music while text is on screen.
- Fuser's colour code for stems (drums, bass, lead, vocals) is already familiar to some players. Matching djmanzo's stem colours on the website to its app would help, but only if the app's own colours are kept consistent. Check the app first.
- Topics with no widely known interactive web teacher that I could find: beatmatching (nudging tempo and phase by ear), phrasing (8/16/32-bar structure), DJ EQ swaps (cutting one deck's bass while bringing in the other), and an interactive Camelot wheel. These are djmanzo's openings.
- Accuracy notes for the site's copy. These are standard theory that I did **not** source in this pass, so check them before publishing:
  - A and B on the wheel are minor and major, and the same number pairs relative keys.
  - Each step around the wheel is a perfect fifth, so +7 steps raises the key one semitone and +2 steps raises it a whole tone. These are the "energy boost" moves some DJs use.
  - Most house and techno phrases are 8, 16 or 32 bars of 4/4.
  - Pitching a track ±6% shifts its key by roughly a semitone unless key-lock is on.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch musictheory.net, Bartosz Ciechanowski's essays, Melodics or Yousician pages, Rocksmith, or Beat Saber, so this pass has no cited teaching details for them. (Ciechanowski has published no music essay that I could confirm.)
- I found no interactive web explainer dedicated to beatmatching, phrasing or EQ mixing to cite. That this is a gap is an inference from search results, not something proven.
- I have no data showing that interactive explainers convert visitors into product downloads.

## (b) How do DJ and music software brands market themselves and onboard users?

### Takeaway
Free entry tiers are standard, but each is gated by hardware or by use: VirtualDJ is free at home without a controller, Serato DJ Lite works with Lite controllers, rekordbox Free leads towards the club-standard Pioneer/AlphaTheta ecosystem, djay is free with a PRO subscription for AI features, and Mixxx is fully open source. Education is shifting toward community-partnered courses (AlphaTheta with FEMME HOUSE) and interactive browser lessons (Ableton). The design opposite of neon-and-laser DJ marketing is Teenage Engineering: quiet page chrome, with the product supplying the colour.

### Cited Findings
**Free tiers and their gates**
- VirtualDJ's free licence covers personal, non-professional use without a DJ controller or mixer. Using a controller at home, or using it professionally, needs a paid Home, Pro or Business licence. — [DJ.Studio 2026 buying guide](https://dj.studio/blog/budget-dj-software-beginners-amateur); [DJ.Studio amateur guide](https://dj.studio/blog/amateur-dj-software-affordable-beginner-friendly)
- Serato DJ Lite is free for beginners with Lite-compatible controllers; recording and advanced functions need an upgrade. rekordbox Free is useful mainly as preparation for the Pioneer/AlphaTheta gear "you'll find in most club booths". — [DJ.Studio](https://dj.studio/blog/amateur-dj-software-affordable-beginner-friendly)
- Mixxx is described as the only fully open-source option, with "no hidden costs and no trial clock". — [OBSBOT blog](https://www.obsbot.com/blog/music-production/best-free-dj-software)
- (These are third-party blogs; vendor terms change often.)

**djay (Algoriddim)**
- PRO features include Neural Mix, Automix, sampler and looper, crossfader FX, recording, and four-deck mode. There is a seven-day trial of djay Pro. — [Algoriddim help](https://help.algoriddim.com/topic/first-steps/free-vs-pro); [Algoriddim Windows FAQ](https://help.algoriddim.com/hc/en-us/articles/11269681416476-What-is-the-difference-between-the-free-version-and-the-PRO-subscription-of-djay-Pro-for-Windows)
- Neural Mix Pro launched in 2020 as a separate product for extracting acapellas and instrumentals. — [EDM.com](https://edm.com/gear-tech/algoriddim-neural-mix-pro-launch)

**Education hubs and partnerships**
- AlphaTheta and FEMME HOUSE launched a four-session online "Intro to DJing" course, taught by Gera Akate on a pay-what-you-can basis. It covers preparing music in rekordbox, song structure, phrasing, beatmatching, cue points, transitions, effects and energy control. It is built around the DDJ-FLX4, and FLX2 owners can follow the basics. — [Interspace Music, Aug 2026](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/alphatheta-and-femme-house-launch-intro-to-djing-online-course/); [Rekkerd](https://rekkerd.org/alphatheta-and-femme-house-launch-online-intro-to-djing-course-for-aspiring-djs/); [Mix Online](https://www.mixonline.com/the-wire/alphatheta-and-femme-house-launch-online-intro-to-djing-course-for-aspiring-djs)
- Earlier, Pioneer DJ and The DJ Coach gave DDJ-200 and DDJ-400 owners a free five-day WeDJ course and a seven-day rekordbox 6 course (probably 2020). — [MMR](https://mmrmagazine.com/article.php?id=73032)
- Local free workshops with networking on Pioneer gear also exist, for example one run by the Institute of Sound in Turkey. — [Radar Türkiye](https://www.radarturkiye.com/en/event/free-dj-workshop-alphatheta-pioneer-turkiye-Bly4wK)

**Gamified practice apps used as onboarding**
- Melodics uses streaks, trophies and achievements, gives real-time feedback on timing and accuracy, teaches hip hop, house, trap, funk and lo-fi on pads, keys and drums, and offers a seven-day premium desktop trial. — [Melodics official info](https://melodics.com/info/ai-info-page)
- Yousician gamifies with rewards, high scores and levels. A Guitar World review praised how it motivates practice but called the learning "somewhat superficial", and its microphone "hears the result, not the technique". — [Violet Recording review](https://violetrecording.com/yousician-review/); [TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/apps/review-yousician-guitar-gamifies-learning-chords-and-riffs/)

**Visual language: Teenage Engineering as the counter-example**
- A design-system write-up says the marketing site "refuses to sell". It shows a product photo on a dark background, a small lowercase wordmark, and chrome limited to black, white and three greys: "the products supply the color". — [shadcn.io design write-up](https://www.shadcn.io/design/teenage-engineering/raw)
- The product is the hero, shown as a 3D render you can rotate, with specs below rather than over it. — [Blake Crosley design guide](https://blakecrosley.com/es/guides/design/teenage-engineering)
- A fan study draws the brand keywords "playful, tactile, curious, purposeful complexity" from the product line. This is not an official statement. — [Yanko Design, Jan 2026](https://www.yankodesign.com/2026/01/12/mp-1-reimagines-a-modern-walkman-through-a-teenage-engineering-lens/amp/)

### Inferences
- djmanzo's edition ladder (free Hobbyist up to Full) sits in a crowded space of free tiers. What it can honestly claim to differ on, per its own feature list, is stems, an AI teaching assistant and karaoke hosting in one app. The site should show these working rather than list them. Note that djay keeps stems behind PRO; whether djmanzo's free tier includes stems should be stated plainly, from the app's real licence gates.
- DJ marketing clichés to avoid, from my observation: neon, lasers, crowds with hands in the air, festival main stages, bottle-service glamour, and "become a superstar DJ" promises. The Teenage Engineering approach (quiet frame, the instrument as hero, playful details) and the Ableton approach (teach first, sell second) are the credible alternatives. I did not find a published critique of DJ-marketing clichés to cite, so this point is opinion.
- Partnering with a community collective, as AlphaTheta did with FEMME HOUSE using pay-what-you-can pricing, is a credible and wholesome model for any djmanzo "learn" programme.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the official marketing or onboarding pages for Serato, rekordbox, Traktor/Native Instruments, VirtualDJ, Mixxx, Splice or Roland. Their current 2026 hero copy, videos, trials and education hubs (for example Serato's learning content, or a current Pioneer "DJ Academy") are unverified in this pass.
- I found no current source on whether Neural Mix Pro is still sold, or on djay's 2026 pricing.
- I found no published study on what onboarding converts best for DJ software.

## Karaoke product marketing and culture

### Takeaway
Karaoke apps sell the party, not the software: phones as remotes, guests' photos on screen, a huge catalogue counted in tens of thousands of songs, and offline reliability. Japan's karaoke box (private rooms, and now solo "hitokara") shows karaoke can be intimate and low-pressure, not only a stage in front of strangers.

### Cited Findings
- KaraFun lists about 59,000 songs, with more than 400 added each month. Premium costs $9.99 a month, and a $49-a-month Pro tier is for venues. Party features: guests control it from their phones, send photos that appear under the lyrics, and manage the queue. It also works offline. — [KaraFun subscribe page](https://karafun.com/subscribe.html); [AppFollow listing](https://appfollow.io/ios/karafun-karaoke-singing/431050674?country=il)
- KaraFun's app pushes a prominent Premium tab, a "soft paywall". — [ScreensDesign showcase](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/karafun-karaoke-battle-quiz)
- Singa leads with a free app and full catalogue. Its differentiator is recordings by the original artists, marked "Original". It also offers AirPlay to a TV, themed setlists, a finder for nearby Singa-powered venues, and a 30-day Premium trial. — [Singa, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id802874608)
- Karaoke history: Shigeichi Negishi's 1967 "Sparko Box" is credited as the first commercial karaoke device. Daisuke Inoue made backing tapes and the coin-operated "8 Juke". Neither patented the idea, and Inoue won a 2004 Ig Nobel Peace Prize. — [Rakuten Travel guide](https://travel.rakuten.com/contents/usa/en-us/guide/all-about-karaoke-tokyo/); [Morikami Museum blog](https://blogarchive.morikami.org/?p=5368); [Tokyo Weekender](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/entertainment/tech-trends/history-of-karaoke-in-japan/)
- Karaoke boxes (private rooms) are why many Japanese people sing alone or in small groups rather than in front of strangers. Solo karaoke is growing, served by dedicated venues such as 1Kara with booths for one. — [Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/travel/1kara-solo-karaoke/); [fun-japan](https://fun-japan.jp/en/articles/1389)

### Inferences
- djmanzo's karaoke hosting should be marketed with the host's tools in view: the queue, phones as remotes, singer rotation, and a friendly screen. A "private room" or "solo practice" mode echoes hitokara and lowers stage fright.
- Catalogue size is the karaoke industry's headline number. If djmanzo uses the user's own library plus stem separation instead of a licensed catalogue, the site must say so honestly and not imply a catalogue it does not have.

### Gaps
- I did not research Smule or Latin American and Caribbean karaoke culture (for example karaoke in Dominican colmados, or Puerto Rican and Mexican karaoke bars), so I have no cited material for them.
- I found no source on when hitokara emerged.

## (c) Real-life DJ culture touchpoints: genre maps, lineages, scenes, and wholesome culture

### Takeaway
Two landmark genre maps are frozen: Every Noise at Once stopped updating after Spotify's December 2023 layoffs, and Ishkur's Guide v3 dates from 2019. That leaves room for a living, small, accurate genre map tied to the music djmanzo users actually play. Genre lineage stories are rich but contested; dembow is a good example of a history that has to be told carefully. Sober and morning dance events such as Daybreaker are the "good parts" community model.

### Cited Findings
- Every Noise at Once: Glenn McDonald was among the roughly 1,500 Spotify employees laid off on 4 December 2023. Without data access the site could not be updated. It had about 6,291 genres across around a million artists, and Spotify said its status was likely to stay as it was. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/12/every-noise-shut-down-spotify-layoffs); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Every_Noise_at_Once); [Billboard Canada](https://ca.billboard.com/business/streaming/spotify-s-former-data-alchemist-gives-every-song-a-genre)
- Ishkur's Guide to Electronic Music v3 came out on 20 August 2019, rebuilt without Flash. It covers 166 genres and 11,321 tracks and adds a timeline, descriptions and search. The Guardian called it an "epic online endeavour" but noted that some genres (for example "Buttrock Goa") were invented by Ishkur. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishkur%27s_Guide_to_Electronic_Music); [CDM](https://cdm.link/2019/08/ishkur-guide-electronic-music-v3/); [Reverb](https://reverb.com/news/legendary-interactive-ishkur-guide-to-electronic-music-updated-after-14-years)
- Dembow:
  - The name comes from Jamaican artist Shabba Ranks' 1990 song "Dem Bow" (riddim by Steely & Clevie, first used on Gregory Peck's "Poco Man Jam", 1989). — [Wikipedia: Dem Bow](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Dem_Bow); [Wikipedia: Shabba Ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabba_Ranks)
  - The loop most sampled in reggaeton came from Jamaican and Panamanian collaborators in the early 1990s at Philip Smart's HC&F studio on Long Island. — [Red Bull Music Academy, "Dembow: A Loop History"](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2013/07/dembow-a-loop-history); [Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/node/2559651); [Wayne&Wax](https://wayneandwax.com/?p=7546)
  - The rhythm uses the tresillo pattern found widely in Latin American music. — [Wikipedia: Dembow](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dembow)
  - The original "Dem Bow" lyrics are anti-gay. Any lineage feature must name the riddim, not celebrate the song. — [Wikipedia: Dem Bow](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Dem_Bow)
  - Early Puerto Rican reggaeton was itself called "dembow", which drew on Panamanian Spanish-language reggae, dancehall and hip-hop. — [Wikipedia: Dem Bow](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Dem_Bow); [EPFL Wikispeedia: Reggaeton](https://dlab.epfl.ch/wikispeedia/wpcd/wp/r/Reggaeton.htm)
- Daybreaker:
  - Sober morning dance parties since 2013. They often open with yoga or guided movement, followed by a couple of hours of dancing. The community is reported at more than 500,000 people in about 30 cities, in changing venues such as arts centres, malls and rooftops. — [Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/entrepreneurship-innovation/sober-morning-raves?amp=1); [A Sweat Life](https://asweatlife.com/2015/12/daybreaker-the-morning-dance-rave-revolution/)
  - Attendees describe strangers talking freely without alcohol. Some sources are 2015–16 student press, so prices and scale may be out of date. — [The Occidental News](https://www.theoccidentalnews.com/community/2015/10/20/rise-and-shine-with-530-a-m-sober-rave/2874485); [The Sober Curator](https://thesobercurator.com/wake-up-and-dance-with-daybreaker-the-global-virtual-sober-morning-dance-community-your-newest-healthy-addiction-virtual-sober-dance-party/)
- Community-led DJ education, such as FEMME HOUSE's partnership with AlphaTheta, is a positive scene touchpoint. — [Interspace Music](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/alphatheta-and-femme-house-launch-intro-to-djing-online-course/)

### Inferences
- A djmanzo "genre island" or map should be small, sourced and modest ("one path through the family tree"). Every genre history (dembow, reggaeton, house, techno, hip-hop, bachata, merengue, electro swing) has contested origin claims, and the dembow case shows a popular story ("Dominican origin") can be wrong about the rhythm's roots, even though Dominican dembow is its own later scene.
- Wholesome touchpoints the site could link to or celebrate: morning and sober dance events, community DJ courses, dance classes, and karaoke nights. Career content should be practical (prepping a library, playing a wedding, running a karaoke night) rather than about fame.

### Gaps
- Not researched with sources in this pass: lineages of house (Chicago), techno (Detroit), hip-hop (Bronx), bachata, merengue and electro swing; city and scene maps; venue types; dance-style lineages; DJ career paths. Each needs its own sourced write-up before it goes on the site.
- Dominican dembow (the 2000s Santo Domingo scene) needs its own sources; my search returned only the Jamaican and Panamanian roots.
- I could not confirm whether Every Noise at Once has been revived since early 2024.

## (d) Gamification patterns for learning a creative skill: what motivates without manipulating?

### Takeaway
Streaks work because of loss aversion, and that is exactly the problem: they can drain the joy from the activity and create anxiety. The humane version uses a tiny minimum action, freezes and grace periods, gradual decay rather than a hard reset, milestone celebrations, and copy that reminds you how far you have come instead of threatening what you will lose. For a creative tool, skills you can see grow and things you can share (a mix, a set) matter more than streak counters.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo itself names loss aversion as the reason people keep long streaks going. People feel losses about twice as strongly as equal gains. — [Smashing Magazine, Feb 2026](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-streak-system-ux-psychology/)
- Humane streak design recommendations:
  - Streak freezes, and a 2–3-hour grace window after the deadline.
  - Decay instead of reset (for example, minus 10 days per missed day).
  - Restore streaks broken by bugs, and count days in the user's local time.
  - Make "the required action as small as humanly possible".
  - Celebrate 7, 30, 50, 100 and 365 days, with progress made visible (like GitHub's contribution graph or Apple's Activity rings).
  - "Remind users of how far they have come, not threaten them with what they might lose."
  - Ask whether revenue depends on "selling solutions to anxiety that your product created", because "not all products need a streak system".
  — [Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-streak-system-ux-psychology/)
- Figures cited in that article (secondhand): a streak widget raised commitment by 60%, and a red app-icon badge raised daily active users by 6%. — [Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-streak-system-ux-psychology/)
- "Streak creep": gamification can drain intrinsic enjoyment, and Reddit users describe "Duolingo burnout" and going to great lengths to protect a streak. — [The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification)
- Parents report streak anxiety in children. — [Screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/managing-duolingo-streaks-when-gamification-becomes-stressful)
- Yu-kai Chou proposes recovery-first designs with capped streaks and grace periods, and cites a 2023 *Journal of Consumer Research* study (Silverman & Barasch) showing that broken streaks suppress later engagement. I saw this only secondhand. — [Yu-kai Chou](https://yukaichou.com/?p=38981)
- Melodics (streaks, trophies, timing feedback) and Yousician (scores, levels) are the music-learning reference points. Yousician is criticised for rewarding the audible result rather than technique. — [Melodics](https://melodics.com/info/ai-info-page); [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/yousician-review/)

### Inferences
- For djmanzo, a skill tree whose nodes are real DJ skills (beatmatch by ear, phrase-aligned transition, EQ bass swap, harmonic move, stem-based mashup, hosting a karaoke rotation) beats a daily streak. Each node is "earned" by doing the thing once in the app or on the site, not by logging in.
- Use weekly or "practice sessions this month" counters with no punishment, celebratory milestones, and badges you can share that link to a recorded mix.
- The AI assistant's tone should follow Smashing's rule: celebrate progress, never guilt.
- The Yousician critique applies to DJ apps too. Software can measure beat alignment and key compatibility, but not taste or crowd-reading, so achievements should never claim to grade those.

## (e) Concrete, accurate "learn by touching" ideas for the djmanzo website

### Takeaway
Each moment below takes one concept from the gaps above. It follows the hear → change one control → hear the difference → free-play loop (Ableton, Chrome Music Lab, Fuser), pauses or quietens the music whenever there is text to read (the lesson from Fuser's criticism), and ends in a small shareable result.

### Cited Findings
- The patterns these ideas borrow:
  - A real working instrument instead of video, plus a playground, from Ableton Learn. — [CDM](https://cdm.link/2026/07/ableton-learn/)
  - Grids you can share by link, from Chrome Music Lab's Song Maker. — [Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/news/learning-music-art-science-math-and-more-with-chrome-music-lab)
  - Colour-coded stems that are introduced one at a time, from Fuser. — [Vooks](https://vooks.net/fuser-switch-review)
  - "What comes next" probabilities from Hooktheory Trends. — [Hooktheory](https://hooktheory.com/blog/?p=2246)
  - The Camelot neighbour rule (8A goes to 7A, 9A or 8B). — [Mixed In Key](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-use-the-camelot-wheel/)
  - Humane progress, from Smashing Magazine. — [Smashing](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-streak-system-ux-psychology/)

### Inferences
These are proposals, built on the findings above. Every number in their copy needs checking against the app and against theory before launch.

1. **Tap the tempo.** The visitor taps a key or the screen along with a loop. The site shows the BPM they tapped and how far each tap was from the beat (a dot drifting early or late), then reveals the loop's real BPM. It teaches what BPM is and that "on the beat" is a window, not a point.
2. **Nudge the platter.** Two loops at slightly different tempos audibly "gallop" (flam). The visitor drags a pitch fader until the gallop stops, with a phase meter as an optional hint they can hide. This is beatmatching by ear, the gap no web explainer fills.
3. **Count the phrase.** A track's waveform split into labelled 8-bar blocks. The visitor drops a "mix in here" marker; a marker on a 16 or 32-bar boundary lands cleanly, while one in the middle of a phrase audibly clashes. Use djmanzo's real beat grid, and say on the page that phrase lengths vary by genre.
4. **The Camelot dial.** A playable wheel. Tapping 8A plays a short chord stab and lights up 7A, 9A and 8B as "friends". Dragging a second track around the wheel plays both together, so the visitor hears consonance or clash. A toggle shows standard key names next to the Camelot codes, and the copy credits Mark Davis and Mixed In Key. This is the strongest unique idea: an interactive Camelot toy appears to be missing from the web.
5. **Bass swap.** Two tracks playing over each other with both basslines muddy. The visitor sweeps three EQ knobs and sees a live spectrum (in the style of Chrome Music Lab's Spectrogram) while learning "only one bass at a time".
6. **Filter sweep.** A single high-pass/low-pass knob over a loop, with the spectrum curve moving as it turns. Learning Synths has shown this is best taught one control at a time.
7. **Un-mix it.** A short clip split into colour-coded stems: drums, bass, harmony and vocals, matching the app's colours. Tapping mutes or solos each one. A "make it karaoke" button mutes the vocals and shows lyrics, which ties stems to the karaoke feature.

   Honesty note to put on the page: separation is approximate, and artefacts are audible. Djay restricts stems on streamed music; djmanzo's licence and source limits should be stated plainly.
8. **Eight-pad sampler.** Chop a public-domain or commissioned loop into pads with a grid like Chrome Music Lab's Rhythm, and let the visitor share the pattern by link.
9. **Ask the assistant.** A sandboxed mini-chat where the visitor asks "what should I mix after this?". The assistant answers with a Camelot move and a BPM range, and explains why in one sentence. It mirrors the app's teaching assistant without overselling it.
10. **Autopilot, explained.** A side-by-side timeline showing which choices autopilot made (key, tempo, phrase point, EQ swap). It turns automation into a lesson rather than a black box.
11. **Skill tree, not streaks.** Optional and stored on the device: complete each toy once to light up a node. There is no daily streak and nothing is lost. Milestone confetti appears, plus a "what you've learned" card. On the site it nudges nothing; the app is where people practise.
12. **Living genre map (small and sourced).** A handful of lineages, starting with dembow's Jamaica → Panama → Long Island → Puerto Rico → Dominican Republic path, each step with a citation, a short audio snippet and a "mix two neighbours" button. It honours Every Noise at Once and Ishkur's Guide by name, and says plainly that it is one path, not the truth.
13. **Out in the world.** A "the good parts" panel pointing to sober and morning dance events (Daybreaker-style), community DJ courses (FEMME HOUSE-style), dance classes and karaoke nights, instead of nightlife-excess imagery.
14. **Visual direction.** A quiet frame where the instrument is the hero and colour comes from the interactive parts (the Teenage Engineering approach). Avoid neon, lasers and festival-crowd stock imagery.

Technical caution: in-browser audio toys must start on a user gesture, play no audio by default, and be keyboard-accessible with reduced-motion fallbacks. All audio on the site must be cleared for licensing: commissioned, self-made, or public domain. The project's no-GPL rule (ADR-0002) also applies to any audio or DSP JavaScript library chosen for the website.
