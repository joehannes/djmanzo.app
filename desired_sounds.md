# Desired sounds for the djmanzo website (v3), to generate with Suno

Everything the site would play, in one place: songs with words, instrumental
beds for each place on the site, tracks built to teach, karaoke songs,
seasonal pieces, and the short sounds to cut from them. Each entry gives
what to paste into Suno and why the site wants it.

## How to use this list

- **Make them on a paid Suno plan** (Pro or Premier). Only songs made while
  subscribed come with commercial rights; the research report on version 3
  has the current terms. Keep the song page or receipt of each one you use.
- **Paste three fields per song:** *Styles* (the line under **Style**),
  *Exclude styles* (the line under **Exclude**), and *Lyrics*. For the
  instrumentals, switch **Instrumental** on and leave *Lyrics* empty.
- **No artist names** in a style prompt; Suno refuses them, and the
  descriptions here do not need them.
- **Tempo and key matter.** Each entry names a BPM and a key with its Camelot
  code. The site cross-fades on bar lines and teaches harmonic mixing with
  these songs, so keep the ones that land on, or very near, what is asked
  (djmanzo itself will tell you the BPM and key of each result).
- **Make three or four takes** of each and keep the best. For the beds,
  split the keeper into stems (Suno's own stem export, or djmanzo's stems —
  a fine demo in itself) so the site can add and remove layers.
- **Loudness:** export WAV; the site will level everything to about
  −16 LUFS, so do not chase loudness in Suno.

## What the words are about

The songs carry Christian values as pictures from the booth and the dance
floor: the fruit of the Spirit (love, joy, peace, patience, kindness,
goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control, Galatians 5:22–23), a
few parables, and the good sense of "test everything, hold fast to what is
good" (1 Thessalonians 5:21), which is also the site's own motto: *keep the
good parts*. They smile more than they preach. Hard things (the night that
went too far, pride, the lost son) are seen from a kind distance, with
nobody pointed at, and the turn is always towards discipline and
self-control rather than more.

Each Spanish or German song has a one-line English gloss.
